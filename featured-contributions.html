Featured Contributions

The contributions featured here span system modeling, operational decision-making, and privacy-preserving learning. They provide foundations for the continuing development of my research program.

Digital Twin Modeling and Optimization

Manufacturing performance depends on how production, transport, and buffering interact under uncertainty. Evaluating the system-wide effects of operating alternatives requires detailed models, but their computational cost makes experimentation challenging. I developed a simulation-enabled digital twin framework that connects the states and interactions of manufacturing agents with graph-based analysis of job and vehicle flows to examine process dependencies. Statistical metamodeling for sequential experimentation characterizes how performance varies across operating conditions, supporting the evaluation and optimization of operating alternatives while reducing the simulation effort needed to estimate performance.

Jobshop simulation studies identified inefficient transport patterns and revealed how the dominant bottleneck shifts between transport, machines, and queues under different operating conditions.

This work provides a foundation for my research on representations that make operational interactions and their consequences explicit and analyzable.

Cognitive Digital Twin for Scheduling

Production scheduling must reconcile the differing objectives and constraints of processing, transport, and storage as operating conditions change. I developed a cognitive digital twin framework that equips heterogeneous manufacturing agents with role-specific observations, objectives, and decision-making capabilities. Cognitive Multiagent Scheduling coordinates their task allocations through structured negotiation and a composite reward that connects task transition, workload balance, and transport efficiency with system-wide productivity. This architecture makes trade-offs between local priorities and overall performance explicit within decentralized scheduling.

Jobshop simulations showed how the weighting of role-specific objectives affects congestion and workload distribution. Comparisons with conventional scheduling rules demonstrated improvements in job flow time and, particularly under high congestion, throughput.

This work informs my research on how local decision objectives and coordination mechanisms shape system-level behavior and performance.

Self-Organizing Machine Networks

Disruptions can leave a factory’s physical layout poorly matched to changing material-flow requirements, creating transport bottlenecks and production delays. I developed the Self-Organizing Machine Network (SOMN) framework, in which machine agents learn coordinated policies for adjusting their positions and orientations through multi-agent reinforcement learning. Within a digital twin environment, agents execute these policies in a decentralized manner, accounting for flow relationships, queue accessibility, transport connectivity, and spatial constraints. This connects local spatial actions with system-level performance, enabling layout reconfiguration as an adaptive response to disruption.

Jobshop simulations with transport obstacles showed that SOMN-reconfigured layouts reduced weighted transport distances relative to the original obstructed layouts and could restore production performance to levels close to the unobstructed benchmark.

My ongoing work investigates how to account for relocation effort and evaluate the effects of reconfiguration on system adaptation and recovery over time.

This work informs my research on structural adaptation and its consequences for operational-system behavior and performance.

Privacy-Preserving Neural Networks

Predictive models derive their utility from learning relationships among variables. These learned relationships can also be exploited through model inversion to infer confidential attributes, even when raw data are not shared. I developed Mosaic Neuron Perturbation (MNP), a neural-network training method that distinguishes sensitive and nonsensitive features and applies stronger perturbation to input neurons associated with designated sensitive features. This makes the allocation of protection an explicit design choice, balancing resistance to information reconstruction with predictive utility. The work combines differential privacy analysis with empirical evaluation of inversion risk and extends to multi-party learning through local model training and parameter aggregation.

Experiments using real CNC turning data showed reduced accuracy of sensitive-attribute reconstruction under the evaluated white-box attacks while retaining high predictive accuracy, including in multi-party models.

This work informs my research on how privacy requirements shape learning mechanisms and the evidence needed to evaluate their protection and utility.
